Chemistry

Chemistry Summary

Chemistry is the study of matter, its structure, properties, and how substances react with one another. It helps explain the world around us, from atoms and molecules to fuels, acids, salts, and biological processes.

Core Idea

Atoms

All matter is made of atoms, which contain protons, neutrons, and electrons.

Bonding

Atoms Connect

Atoms form bonds to create molecules and compounds through ionic, covalent, or metallic bonding.

Reactions

Change

Chemical reactions rearrange atoms and produce new substances with different properties.

Applications

Life & Industry

Chemistry is used in medicine, agriculture, energy, materials, and the environment.

1. Matter and Atomic Structure

Atoms

An atom is the smallest unit of an element that still retains the chemical properties of that element. Atoms are made of:

  • Protons: positively charged, in nucleus
  • Neutrons: neutral, in nucleus
  • Electrons: negatively charged, orbiting nucleus

Atomic Number and Mass Number

Atomic number = number of protons. Mass number = protons + neutrons.

Atomic number = Z
Mass number = A = protons + neutrons

Isotopes

Isotopes are atoms of the same element with the same atomic number but different mass numbers. They differ in neutron count.

2. Periodic Table

Arrangement

Elements are arranged in order of increasing atomic number. Groups are vertical columns and periods are horizontal rows.

Groups

Elements in the same group often have similar chemical properties because they have the same number of valence electrons.

Important Groups

  • Group 1: Alkali metals
  • Group 17: Halogens
  • Group 18: Noble gases

3. Chemical Bonding

Ionic Bond

Formed by transfer of electrons. One atom loses electrons and becomes a cation; another gains electrons and becomes an anion.

Example: NaCl

Covalent Bond

Formed by sharing electrons between atoms. Common in nonmetals.

Example: H₂O, CO₂, CH₄

Metallic Bond

Positive metal ions are attracted to a “sea” of delocalized electrons, giving metals conductivity and malleability.

4. States of Matter

Solid

Particles are closely packed and vibrate in fixed positions.

Liquid

Particles are close but can move around each other, so liquids flow.

Gas

Particles are far apart and move freely, filling the container.

Changes of State

  • Solid → Liquid: melting
  • Liquid → Gas: boiling/evaporation
  • Gas → Liquid: condensation
  • Liquid → Solid: freezing
  • Solid → Gas: sublimation

Key Idea

Matter changes state when heat energy is added or removed, changing the movement of particles.

5. Acids, Bases, and pH

Acids

Acids produce H⁺ ions in water. They turn blue litmus red and often taste sour.

Example: HCl, H₂SO₄

Bases

Bases produce OH⁻ ions in water or accept protons. They turn red litmus blue.

Example: NaOH, KOH

pH Scale

pH measures acidity or alkalinity. 7 is neutral.

pH < 7 = acidic
pH = 7 = neutral
pH > 7 = alkaline

Neutralization

An acid reacts with a base to form salt and water:

Acid + Base → Salt + Water

6. Chemical Reactions

Reactants and Products

Reactants are the starting substances, and products are the new substances formed after reaction.

Balanced Equations

A balanced equation has the same number of each type of atom on both sides.

Example: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

Types of Reactions

  • Combination
  • Decomposition
  • Displacement
  • Neutralization
  • Combustion

7. Solutions and Concentration

Solution

A homogeneous mixture of solute dissolved in solvent.

Solubility

Solubility is the maximum amount of solute that can dissolve in a given solvent at a certain temperature.

Concentration

Concentration tells how much solute is present in a given volume of solution.

8. Mole Concept and Stoichiometry

Mole

A mole is a counting unit for particles. 1 mole = 6.022 × 10²³ particles.

1 mol = 6.022 × 10²³ particles

Molar Mass

Molar mass is the mass of one mole of a substance in grams per mole.

Stoichiometry

Stoichiometry is the calculation of amounts of substances involved in a chemical reaction using balanced equations.

9. Organic Chemistry

Organic Compounds

Organic chemistry studies carbon-containing compounds such as hydrocarbons, alcohols, acids, and esters.

Hydrocarbons

Compounds made only of carbon and hydrogen. Examples: methane, ethene, ethyne.

Functional Groups

Functional groups determine chemical properties.

  • Alcohol: -OH
  • Carboxylic acid: -COOH
  • Ester: -COOR

10. Electrolysis and Redox

Oxidation

Gain of oxygen, loss of hydrogen, or loss of electrons.

Reduction

Loss of oxygen, gain of hydrogen, or gain of electrons.

Electrolysis

Electrolysis uses electricity to break down a substance, often an ionic compound or solution.

11. Rates of Reaction

Factors

  • Temperature
  • Concentration
  • Surface area
  • Catalyst

Collision Theory

Reactions occur when particles collide with enough energy and proper orientation.

Catalyst

A catalyst speeds up a reaction by lowering the activation energy, without being used up.

12. Environmental Chemistry

Pollution

Pollution occurs when harmful substances enter air, water, or soil.

Acid Rain

Acid rain is caused by sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides reacting with water in the atmosphere.

Green Chemistry

Green chemistry aims to reduce waste and pollution by using safer and more sustainable chemical processes.

13. Important Chemical Formulae

pH = -log[H⁺]
Acid + Base → Salt + Water
Metal + Acid → Salt + Hydrogen gas
2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

14. Quick Revision Points

Key Ideas

  • Atoms are the building blocks of matter.
  • Electrons determine bonding and chemical behavior.
  • Acids, bases, and salts are important chemical groups.
  • Balanced equations follow the law of conservation of mass.

Study Tips

  • Memorize common ions and formulas.
  • Learn periodic trends and groups.
  • Practice balancing equations.
  • Understand reaction types and bond formation.

Important Rule

Chemistry is about understanding how particles behave, how they connect, and how they change during reactions.
